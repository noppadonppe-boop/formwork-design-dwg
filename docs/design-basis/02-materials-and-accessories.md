## 1. Lumber (ไม้แปรรูป) — §3.2

### 1.1 General / sizes (text)
- Most common formwork timber: **ไม้กระบาก (Krabak)** and **ไม้ยาง (Yang)**; generally softwood ("ไม้เนื้ออ่อน"); medium/hardwood used where strength needed.
- Sheathing boards: thickness **3/4" or 1"**, widths **4", 6", 8"**. Other members commonly **1½" × 3"** and **2" × 4"**.
- Lengths sold in metres / half-metres; formwork usually uses lengths ≤ **6.00 m**; most used **2.00–4.00 m**.
- **Actual (dressed) size reduction**: boards ≤ 6" wide lose about **3/8"** when planed; ≥ 8" lose about **1/2"** (planed 2 faces). Formwork lumber is normally *not* planed unless architectural finish is required.

## 2. Plywood (ไม้อัด) — §3.3

### 2.1 General
- Use for contact surface only; framing in lumber. Edges may be protected with hardwood or steel angle. Treat with oil or lacquer to reduce water absorption; replace when swollen/wavy/delaminated.
- Sheet size **4 × 8 ft (1.22 × 2.44 m)**. Two production types: interior and exterior (exterior only ~5 % of production). Face veneer usually Yang; may be Mahogany, Champa, Yomhom, Yomhin, Teak.
- Three grades: special (extra-good), good, and with-defects; for ordinary formwork the "good" grade suffices.

### 2.4 Design equations (APA-based), allowable concrete pressure on plywood

**(ก) Bending** — Eq. 3.1
`W_b = K_b · f_b · S_e / l_b²`
- W_b = allowable concrete pressure on plywood (kg/cm²)
- f_b = allowable bending stress of plywood (kg/cm²)
- S_e = section modulus of a 1-unit-wide strip (cm³)
- l_b = span, centre-to-centre of supports (cm)
- K_b = continuity factor: **8** for 2 supports (single span); **10** for 3 or more supports (continuous)

**(ข) Bending deflection** — Eq. 3.2
`W_d = K_d · E · I · D_b / l_d⁴`
- D_b = allowable deflection (cm); E = modulus of plywood (kg/cm²); I = moment of inertia of 1-unit strip (cm⁴); l_d = span (cm)
- K_d = **185** for 2 supports; **145** for 3 or more supports

**(ค) Rolling/planar shear** — Eq. 3.3
`W_s = K_s · f_s · (Ib/Q) / l_s`
- f_s = allowable (rolling) shear stress (kg/cm²)
- Ib/Q = shear constant per 1-unit strip (cm²); printed "for rectangular section = 3/2" (⚠ NOTE: for a solid rectangle Ib/Q = 2t/3 per unit width; the "3/2" is likely a garbled "2t/3")
- K_s = **1.60** for 2 supports; **1.67** for 3 or more supports

**(ง) Shear deflection** — Eq. 3.4
`W_s = K_sd · D_s · E_e · I / (C · t² · l_s²)`
- D_s = allowable deflection (cm); t = plywood thickness (cm); E_e = modulus adjusted for shear deflection (kg/cm²)
- C = grain factor: **2** when face grain ⊥ supports; **1** when face grain ∥ supports
- K_sd = **32** for rectangular section

Allowable deflection commonly **l/360**.

⚠ The book gives **no per-thickness table of I, S_e, Ib/Q** for plywood. For a homogeneous approximation per 1 cm width: I = t³/12, S = t²/6, Ib/Q = 2t/3. (APA "effective" values for cross-ply would differ for parallel vs perpendicular face grain — not provided.)

**Withdrawal — Eq. 4.1** (nail driven ⊥ to grain; **includes FS = 6**)
`P = 95 · G^(5/2) · D · H`
P = allowable withdrawal (kg); G = specific gravity of wood (oven-dry weight & volume); D = nail diameter (cm); H = penetration depth into member (cm).

**Lateral — Eq. 4.2** (nail ⊥ to grain; **includes FS = 6**)
`P = K · D^(3/2)`
P = allowable lateral load (kg); D = nail diameter (cm); K = species coefficient **121–229**.
- Valid when members joined have similar density; penetration must exceed **10 D** (dense wood) and **14 D** (light wood).
- Nails driven **parallel to grain** (end grain): reduce both withdrawal and lateral allowables to **60 %**.
- In **plywood**: lateral capacity reduce **20–30 %** from solid-wood values (variable grain direction through thickness).

### 7.4 Form ties (เหล็กยึดรั้ง) — §4.5
- Traditional: **6 mm or 9 mm** bars through both forms, twisted/nailed — poor thickness control; protruding steel rusts.
- Proprietary types (Fig. 4.4): snap tie with small cone spreader; washer spreader crimped for break-back (optional water seal); wood cone spreader; no-spreader (pull-out or break points); taper tie (withdrawn); strap tie (panels); loop-end tie (panels); coil-type tie with cone spreader (coil bolt + washer); she-bolt/stud-rod with cone nuts (cold-drawn steel tie rod, square-cut threads, cast-iron nut washer).
- Tightening: wedges, spiral/coil, or bolts; inner unit may be galvanized/painted; some have water-stop flange; cone holes patched with mortar.
- Outer (external) part length = from concrete face to outside of wale + ≈ **1.0 cm**.

### 7.5 Anchors (สมอยึด) — §4.6

- **Safety factor 1.5 – 3** depending on conditions; consult manufacturer data / load test.

### 7.8 Shores (ค้ำยัน) — §4.9
- Components: head (top plate), post, height adjuster, base plate. Main problem is **buckling** → brace at mid-height or between lifts.
- **Timber shores**: preferably 2 pieces spliced with nails (adjustable); head plate with **1½" × 3"** knee braces forming a triangle (T-head); base on boards/sole plates to spread load; brace both horizontally and diagonally.
- **Steel adjustable shores**: telescoping tubes, pin through holes and/or screw collar for fine adjustment; base may have hole for dowel to ground.
- **Scaffold frames used as shoring**: frame heights **1.20, 1.50, 1.80, 2.00, 2.40 m** (manufacturer-dependent); adjustable U-head (top) and screw jack (base). Capacity governed by leg strength — follow manufacturer data, check stability and tie to adjacent structure.
- Fig. 4.14 (load-test failure modes): towers 4′ wide × 8′ braced bays, 20′ high in 6′ lifts with 12″ extensions top/bottom, and 19′ (5′+6′+6′ lifts) — buckling as single sway, partial, or multi-wave modes.
- **No shore/prop capacity-vs-height table in the book.**

### 7.9 Rebar spacers/chairs (หมอนหนุนเหล็กเสริม) — §4.10
- Mortar blocks cast to cover thickness **2, 3, 4, 5 cm**; for **7, 10, 12 cm** use fine-aggregate concrete; 2 tie wires (or 2–3 pairs for tall blocks). Block strength ≥ structural concrete strength.
- Steel chairs (individual / continuous), tips coated with ceramic/plastic against rust; plastic spacers/clips.
