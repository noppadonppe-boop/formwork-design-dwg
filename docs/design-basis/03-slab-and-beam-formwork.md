## Part A — Slab formwork (Chapter 6)

### A.1 Scope and slab types (6.1)
- Slab systems: slab on beams (one-way, two-way, or cantilever), flat slab/flat plate, waffle slab, joist slab,
  slab on grade, and slabs between precast beams. The most common is the slab on beams. Beam and slab are ideally cast monolithically,
  so the slab form must be coordinated with the beam form (load transfer and connections, easy stripping, no honeycombing).
- Flat slabs (no beams) are simplest to form. Waffle and joist slabs use prefabricated pans.
- Slab on grade: forms only at edges. The subgrade must be compacted, leveled, watered, and covered with plastic sheet or lean concrete to stop
  water loss and rebar sinking.
- Precast joist + infill systems: the joists cannot carry full load until the topping reaches strength, so temporary shoring is needed.
- This chapter covers only ordinary slabs on beams and flat slabs. Special forms are in Ch. 11.

### A.2 Component hierarchy (6.2), Fig. 6.1 and 6.2

Load path: **sheathing (แผ่นผิว) → joists (ตง) *or* เคร่า (two-way battens) → stringers (คานรองรับ) → shores (เสาตู้)
→ base plates/sole (แป้นรองฐาน) → floor below**, plus **lateral bracing (ค้ำยันทางข้าง)** on the shores and
**edge forms (ข้างแบบ)** at slab edges.

| Thai | English | Function / notes |
|---|---|---|
| แผ่นผิว | Sheathing / decking | Mostly plywood. Formerly 1"×8" or ¾"×8" lumber boards on joists, but these warp and leak at the joints, so the finished surface needs plastering. Sheathing thickness depends on joist (or เคร่า) spacing. |
| ตง | Joist | Carries sheathing load to the stringers. With joists the sheathing acts **one-way**. Suits ordinary slabs and flat slabs. |
| เคร่า | Batten / sheathing-stiffener grid (Fig 6.2) | Used instead of joists. The sheathing then acts **two-way (plate)**, which gives less deflection and more capacity. Sheathing and เคร่า are often prefabricated as panels. Popular for flat slabs. |
| คานรองรับ | Stringer (ledger beam) | Receives the joists/เคร่า and delivers load to the shores. The joist load is treated as point loads or as a UDL, as appropriate. Size depends on shore spacing and joist load. |
| เสาตู้ | Shore / post | Delivers load to the floor below. Capacity is governed by size and slenderness. Lateral bracing may be needed to reduce slenderness and prevent buckling. |
| แป้นรองฐาน | Base plate / sole plate | Placed under shores when the supporting surface is weak. Also aids stripping: knock it out and the shore drops a little, freeing the rest. |
| ค้ำยัน(ทางข้าง) | Lateral bracing | Prevents lateral buckling and raises shore capacity by reducing slenderness. Can be one direction if one face is weaker. Square or round shores need bracing in **both** directions. |
| ข้างแบบ | Edge form | Slab edge form with edge stiffener and cleats at intervals to prevent bulging. Nailed to the เคร่า/sheathing or the stringers. |

Figure 6.1 (joist system, described): plywood sheathing on joists. The joists sit perpendicular on stringers (shown as a timber beam on top of
each shore). Shores are vertical posts with a horizontal brace (ค้ำยัน) at about mid-height in elevation, and each shore stands on a
small base plate (แป้นรองฐาน). An edge form (ข้างแบบ) is shown at the slab edge on top of the sheathing line.
Figure 6.2 (เคร่า system): sheathing on a grid of เคร่า, carried by double stringers (คานรองรับ) clamping the shore heads (เสาตู้).

### A.3 Assembly (6.3)
**Modular spacing.** Joist/เคร่า spacing should match the sheathing panel size so panels can be reused without re-cutting.
Common spacings are **15, 20, 30, 40, 60, 120 cm**, whatever the sheathing (plywood, lumber or steel). Panel weight
should let **2 workers** lift it without equipment. Panels are site-specific or company-wide standard.

Panel layout principles:
1. Maximize use of standard panels.
2. Make leftover pieces match one dimension of the standard panel (half-standard panels).
3. Minimize filler pieces.
4. Put filler strips in the middle strip or the outermost strip of the slab.

Stringers run in one direction. Where fillers exist, add stringers or shores as needed. Shores and stringers
must suit the concrete thickness, the equipment and the formwork self-weight. Design calculation is required, but ease of
construction and stripping also governs.

**Erection sequence (slab):**
1. Snap lines in both directions to locate the shores.
2. Erect the shores and check the spacing in both directions.
3. Brace the shores temporarily with lateral bracing so they stay aligned and plumb.
4. Put a block of 1½"×3" or 2"×4" lumber, about 10–20 cm long, under **every** shore to make stripping possible. Screw-jack shores don't need this.
5. Install the stringers and check the level. Members must be straight and unwarped, and must be repaired before reuse.
6. Stringers adjacent to beam forms must have a ledger/cleat or be nailed to the beam side-form studs.
7. Lay the standard, half-standard and filler panels as drawn. Joints must be tight and flush. Use wedges to adjust the level.
   Tape or fill wide gaps.
8. Assemble the edge forms with edge stiffeners and cleats, fixed to the เคร่า/joists so they cannot slide or bulge.
9. Construction-joint bulkheads (กั้นแบบ) need a shear key. Where rebar passes through, use a comb-shaped or drilled bulkhead.

Then inspect all joints for grout leaks. Add blockouts for openings, pipes and conduits without disturbing the main system.

**Stripping sequence (slab).** Check the concrete age and the reshoring requirement first.
1. Remove the edge forms by pulling the nails at the joists/เคร่า.
2. Remove the lateral bracing strip by strip. Stack lumber and steel in groups immediately.
3. Knock out the wedges/blocks under the shores. Shores and stringers drop onto the slab below, and the sheathing separates from the concrete.
4. Standard, half and filler panels drop onto the stringers.
5. If possible, pull the sheathing off first so it doesn't fall to the floor. The first strips may require removing shores and stringers first
   to create room. Later panels are then easier.
6. If reshoring is required, reshore strip by strip using base plates and wedges, driven tight.
7. Strip the rest strip by strip. Sort the components, then clean, repair and oil them for reuse.

Flat slabs: strip from the sides toward the middle. Slabs on beams: strip the filler strip first (usually the middle strip), then work toward the
beams. Beam bottoms normally stay in place longest, as the construction spec requires.

#### A.4.3 Beam formulas used (continuous over more than 3 spans, uniform load)
- Bending: **M = w·l²/10** [Table 5.3]. Section: **b·d² = 6M/Fb** [eq 5.7] (rectangular S = bd²/6).
  With w in kg/m and l in m: **l = √(b·d²·Fb / (60·w)) [m]** (b, d in cm).
- Shear: **V = 5/8·w·l** [eq 5.9]. **v = 3V/(2bd)** [eq 5.11]. Setting v = H gives **l = (16/15)·b·d·H / w** (w in kg/cm, l in cm).
- Deflection: **Δmax = w·l⁴/(128·E·I)** [eq 5.28]. With Δ = l/360: **l = 0.71·∛(E·I/w)** [eq 5.30] (w in kg/cm, l in cm).
  (Exact constant (128/360)^(1/3) = 0.7086.)
- Simple span (used for the beam cross-head on double posts, Ex 7.3): M = w·l²/8, V = w·l/2.

**Spacing solve:** compute the allowable span from each check (bending, shear, deflection). Take the minimum, then round **down**
to a practical/modular value (15/20/30/40/60/75/80/90/120 …).

#### A.4.4 Plate (two-way) check for sheathing on เคร่า: Ricouard formulas
- Bending: **M = k₁·w·a²** [eq 5.1], b·d² = 6M/Fb, where b = panel width and a = เคร่า spacing.
- Deflection: **a = 0.14·h·∛(E / (k₂·w))** [eq 5.32]. h = sheathing thickness (cm), E in kg/m² (E[kg/cm²] × 10,000), w in kg/m².
- Coefficients depend on b/a. The only value given in this chapter: b/a = 1.6 → k₁ = 0.0853, k₂ = 0.0896.
  (The full k₁/k₂ table is in Chapter 5.)

#### A.4.5 Shore (column) capacity
- Wood shore: **Fa = 0.30·E / (l/d)²** [eq 5.15], P = Fa·A [eq 5.16]. d = least dimension. l = unbraced (effective) length.
  For a round log, d = diameter.
- Steel pipe shore: r = √(OD² + ID²)/4. A = π/4·(OD² − ID²). For **l/r ≥ 132: Fa = 10,000,000/(l/r)²** kg/cm² [eq 5.18],
  P = Fa·A [eq 5.19]. (The l/r < 132 formula is in Ch. 5. Table 6.11 marks those entries with superscript 1.)
- Shore load = w(area) × stringer spacing × shore spacing (tributary area).
- Mid-height lateral bracing halves l in the braced direction. The examples brace the weak axis and check l/d with d = the thin dimension.
- Bearing perpendicular to grain at the stringer/shore contact is **not** computed in Ch. 6/7. The text only says the stringer tables assume
  "adequate bearing area". Treat it as a separate check (see Ch. 5).

#### A.4.6 Step-by-step slab design (book procedure)
1. Loads: w (kg/m²) = 2400·t + LL(150) + forms(50).
2. **Sheathing → joist spacing a.** Plywood: take a strip b = 120 cm (lumber: b = 100 cm). Line load = w·b/100 (kg/cm per strip).
   Compute a_bending, a_deflection (and the plate check for a เคร่า system). Take the min and round down. Tables 6.1–6.3.
3. **Joist → stringer spacing l.** Joist line load = w × a (kg/m). Compute l from bending, deflection (and shear). Take the min and round down. Table 6.6 / 6.7.
4. **Stringer → shore spacing L.** Stringer load = w × l (kg/m), treated as a UDL. Compute from shear, bending and deflection. Take the min and round. Table 6.8 / 6.9.
5. **Shore check.** P = w × l × L. Compare with Fa·A for the height (or Table 6.10/6.11). If inadequate, add mid-height bracing or use a bigger shore.
6. Lateral bracing: provide it in both directions for square/round shores. Base plates and wedges as A.3.

### A.6 Worked examples — Chapter 6 (unit tests)

#### Example 6.1: Slab on a 7.20 × 7.20 m panel (column grid, flat slab type)
**Given:** storey height 2.80 m. Slab t = 16 cm. LL during work 150 kg/m². Plywood sheathing 20 mm. Joists ไม้ยาง (rubber wood) 1½"×3".
Stringers ไม้ยาง 2"×6". Shores steel pipe Ø2" thin wall (แบบบาง).

**Method ก: by formula**
1. Loads: concrete 0.16×2,400 = **384**. LL **150**. Forms **50**. Total w = **584 kg/m²**.
2. Plywood → joist spacing. Fb = 135, E = 115,500. Beam behaviour (ACI 347), strip width 120 cm, spacing a:
   - Bending: M = wa²/10 = (584×1.2/100)·a²/10 kg-cm. 120×2² = (6/135)(584×1.2/100)(a²/10) → **a = 124 cm**.
   - Deflection (Δ = l/360, eq 5.30): a = 0.71∛(115,500 × (1/12)×120×2³ / (584×1.2/100)) → **a = 77 cm**. Deflection governs, so use **75 cm**.
   - Plate check (Ricouard): b = 120, a = 75, b/a = 1.6, k₁ = 0.0853, k₂ = 0.0896.
     Bending: M = 0.0853×584·a² (kg-m). 120×2² = (6/135)×0.0853×584·a²×100 → **a = 1.47 m (147 cm)**.
     Deflection: a = 0.14×2×∛(115,500×10,000/(0.0896×584)) → **a = 78 cm**.
   - **Joist spacing = 75 cm.**
3. Joist (1½"×3" @ 0.75 m) → stringer spacing. I = (1/12)×3.75×7.5³ = **131 cm⁴**. W = 584×0.75 = **438 kg/m**. Fb = 80, E = 94,100.
   - Bending: l = √(3.75×7.5²×80/(60×438)) = **0.80 m**.
   - Deflection: l = 0.71∛(94,100×131/(438/100)) = **100 cm**.
   - **Stringer spacing = 80 cm.**
4. Stringer (2"×6" @ 0.80 m) → shore spacing. w = 584×0.80 = **467 kg/m = 4.67 kg/cm**. Fb = 80, H = 8, E = 94,100. Section 5×15 cm.
   - Shear: l = (16/15)×(5×15/4.67)×8 = **137 cm**.
   - Bending: l = √(5×15²×80/(60×467)) = **1.79 m**.
   - Deflection: I = 5×15³/12 = **1,406 cm⁴**. l = 0.71∛(94,100×1,406/4.67) = **216 cm**.
   - **Shore spacing = 135 cm** (shear governs).
5. Shore Ø2" thin, h = 2.80 m, no mid-height bracing. P = 584×0.80×1.35 = **630 kg**.
   OD = 6.03 cm, ID = 5.45 cm. r = √(5.45² + 6.03²)/4 = **2.03 cm**. A = π/4(6.03² − 5.45²) = **5.23 cm²**.
   l/r = 280/2.03 = **138** (≥ 132). Fa = 10,000,000/138² = **525 kg/cm²**. P_allow = 525×5.23 = **2,745 kg > 630 OK**.

**Method ข: using tables**
1. w = 584 kg/m².
2. Table 6.2, 20 mm plywood, use the 600 kg/m² row → **75 cm**.
3. Joist load 584×0.75 = 438 kg/m. Table 6.6, 1½"×3", use the 450 row → **80 cm**.
4. Stringer load 584×0.80 = 467 kg/m. Table 6.8ก, 2"×6" (single): 450 → 140, 500 → 125. Interpolate 140 − (17/50)(140 − 125) = **135 cm**.
5. P = 630 kg. Table 6.11, 2" L, use effective height 3.00 m → **2,395 kg > 630 OK**.

Note: in practice choose modular spacings. Here the เคร่า/joists might be at **0.60 m**, with the other components unchanged.

#### Example 6.2: Slab on beams, panel 5.00 × 3.50 m
**Given:** slab t = 10 cm. LL 150 kg/m². Sheathing ไม้กะบาก boards ¾"×8". Joists ไม้ยาง 1½"×3". Stringers ไม้ยาง 2"×6".

**Method ก: by formula**

2. Boards ¾"×8" → joist spacing. Fb = 80, E = 94,100. Strip b = 100 cm, d = ¾" = **1.8 cm**, w = **4.40 kg/cm**.

3. Joist 1½"×3" @ 0.70 m. I = 131 cm⁴ (printed as "1/12×75×7.5³", meaning 3.75). W = 440×0.70 = **308 kg/m**.
   - Bending: l = √(3.75×7.5²×80/(60×308)) = **0.95 m**.

## Part B — Beam formwork (Chapter 7)

### B.1 Behaviour and beam-form types (7.1)
The beam form acts like a **slab form for the bottom (vertical loads)** and like a **wall form for the sides (lateral pressure)**. Member
sizes depend on the beam width and depth. Ease of forming depends on the section shape. Rectangular sections are easiest. Trapezoid, L, T and
sections with haunches for precast slabs are harder.

Types:
1. **Isolated beam (คานเดี่ยว)**: no slab or joists framing in. Common for precast-slab buildings or beams cast before the slab. Needs
   top ties or **diagonal braces (ค้ำยันทแยง)** so the sides don't spread. May need a walkway platform or cantilevered panel against falling.
2. **Beam with slab (คานร่วมกับแผ่นพื้น)**: the beam-side top edge is lowered to the slab soffit. The slab joists/เคร่า bear on a **ledger
   (พุก)** on the beam side. The joint needs tight corners (no leaks) and chamfers for easy stripping and neat finish.
3. **Spandrel/edge beam (คานขอบอาคาร)**: inner side like type 2 (monolithic with slab), outer side like an isolated beam. Needs top ties or
   diagonal braces.
4. **Deep beam (คานลึก)**: wall-like. High lateral pressure, so through-**ties (เหล็กยึดรั้ง)** against bulging, plus wall-form components.
5. **Concrete-encased steel beam**: the form is hung from the structural steel beam (fire/corrosion protection). If the slab is cast separately, the top of the form needs ties against bulging.
6. Other shapes (I, T, curved, sloped): special steel or plywood shapes, inclined เคร่า, and secondary beams framing into main beams (side-form openings,
   tight joints).

### B.3 Assembly and stripping (7.3)
Prepare the side panels first: sheathing nailed to studs at the specified spacing, with straight top, bottom and side edges. Start at the shores. T-head
assemblies are built lying down, raised, nailed in rows along the beam, levelled with wedge pairs under the shores, then the
bottom is laid on the cross-beams and aligned with minimal nailing. The bottom edge serves as the kicker. Chamfer strips go on the bottom before the
sides (except for shallow, wide beams where hands can reach). Fitting the sides is easier if the side foot has a horizontal cleat along the beam and
1½"×3" pieces nailed parallel to the bottom, leaving gaps for the side to lock in. Otherwise the studs must be nailed tight against the bottom.
Top width is set with diagonal braces or spreaders. Level is set with wedge pairs on the base plates. Deep beams also get ties.

**Erection sequence (beam):**
1. Assemble the side forms: sheathing and studs nailed into panels as drawn.
2. Assemble the cross-beam, shore and knee braces into a **T-head (ตุ๊กตา)** for single shores, or a **frame** for double shores.
3. Erect the T-heads/frames. Fix the first and last against the columns first, then space the others and brace them laterally to the first and last.
4. Lay the bottom on the cross-beams, align it by fixing to the cross-beams, and level with wedges on the base plates. On soil, check compaction and
   base-plate size.
5. Set the sides tight against the bottom edges and fix them to the cross-beams.
6. Adjust the sides to size. Fix the top with the slab joists or with diagonal braces.
7. Install ties if necessary. Lay walk boards on the cross-beams, especially where there is no slab form.

**Stripping (beam).** Two approaches: (a) strip everything together; (b) strip the sides only and leave the bottom until the concrete is strong enough. Approach (b)
is harder, especially when beam and slab are cast together.

(a) Strip together (bottom reshored if required):
1. Remove the ties (if any).
2. Remove the side braces (if any).
3. Pull the end nails of the slab-joist ledgers (พุกรับตง) and the ledger-to-stud nails. The slab form drops, and the slab is stripped first. If
   reshoring is needed, strip and reshore strip by strip.
4. To keep the beam bottom supported, add a second set of plain shores (no T-head) under the bottom, with enough base area, wedged tight.
5. Pull the nails between the side studs and the cross-beams.
6. Knock out the wedges at the original shores so the T-heads drop, one at a time.
7. Remove the sides.

(b) Strip sides only. Problem: a long T-head cross-beam leaves too little rotation angle to pull the side out. Fix this with shorter cross-beams or extra shores
under the bottom first. Slab joists must **not** bear on the side form, or the side cannot be removed.
1. Remove the ties. 2. Remove the lateral braces. 3. Remove the slab-joist ledgers, keeping the slab shores in place.
4. Pull the nails between the side studs and the cross-beams.
5. Move the sides clear of the cross-beam and strip them. If the angle is insufficient, first add reshores under the bottom (no cross-beam projecting
   beyond the sides), then knock the wedges so the T-heads drop and the sides come straight down.

### B.4 Design procedure (7.4)

#### B.4.1 Loads and pressure
- Lateral pressure on the beam sides: **fluid (hydrostatic) pressure p = γ·h = 2,400·h kg/m²** (h = full beam depth including slab, m).
  General lateral pressure theory is in §2.10.
- Bottom vertical load: concrete = b_beam × h × 2,400 (kg/m), apportioned to the bottom-board width considered.
- Formwork self-weight for the beam form: **100 kg/m** of beam length (Ex 7.1, 7.3, 7.4). Slab form tributary: 50 kg/m².
- Live load 150 kg/m² on the tributary **slab** plan area (Ex 7.2, 7.3). Ex 7.1 and 7.4 include no LL in the shore/hanger load (as printed).
- Caution (text): the shore load must include the formwork weight (bottom, sides, braces) and, when cast with the slab, the tributary slab
  concrete per Fig 7.7.
- Eccentric placing can load the T-head eccentrically, so check the shore for combined bending + axial. Spandrel-beam shores carrying moment need a combined check
  so strength and buckling limits aren't exceeded.

### B.6 Secondary beams framing into girders (7.5 แบบหล่อคานฝาก)
- Main issue: connecting the secondary-beam side forms to the girder. Plan the assembly/stripping order so forms go up and come off quickly with minimal damage and enough strength.
- The secondary-beam bottom needs a cross-beam at its end (Fig 7.10), for both equal and different soffit levels. That cross-beam is fixed to the girder side-form
  studs, or uses a separate T-head if space and shore count allow.
- The secondary side form must butt tightly against the girder side (no leaks or bulging). There are two arrangements (Fig 7.11), chosen by stripping order (see B.2 Fig 7.11).
- Sheathing thickness, stud spacing and shores are designed as for ordinary beams. Only the cross-beam and stud arrangement at the junction need special attention.

### B.7 Concrete-encased structural-steel beams (7.6)
- The steel beam already carries load, so hang both the bottom and the sides from it (Fig 7.5, 7.12). Hangers are spaced at the cross-beam spacing.
- Fig 7.12: (ก) through-threaded hanger (เกลียวสวม), (ข) threaded coupler (เกลียวต่อ).
- If the slab is cast at the same time, slab joists bear on the beam side forms and transfer to the cross-beams. For isolated or spandrel beams, lateral stability is a problem, so brace to
  firm structure on one side. Top bracing options: normal diagonal braces (Fig 7.13), or tie across the top (เย็บปากแบบ, Fig 7.14), leaving enough room to place concrete.

### B.8 Worked examples — Chapter 7 (unit tests)

#### Example 7.1: Isolated beam for precast slab, 30 × 50 cm
**Given:** 15 mm plywood bottom and sides. 1½"×3" studs. Cross-beam with diagonal braces (ค้ำยันทแยง) and a shore (T-head). Assumptions:
concrete 2,400 kg/m³, forms 50 kg/m², LL 150 kg/m². Plywood Fb = 135, E = 115,500. Soft wood Fb = 80, H = 8, E = 94,100. Deflection limit 1/360.

Method I (formula):
1. p = γh = 2,400 × 0.5 = **1,200 kg/m²**.
2. Stud spacing (beam behaviour, ACI-622). Plywood strip 10 cm:

## Part C — Implementation notes for the calculator

1. **Unified span solver** (continuous > 3 spans, UDL): given w (kg/cm), b, d (cm), Fb, H, E:
   - l_b = √(10·Fb·b·d²/(6·w)) [cm] (equivalent to √(bd²Fb/(60w)) m when w is in kg/m)
   - l_v = (16/15)·b·d·H / w [cm] (for rectangular timber; skip for plywood sheathing)
   - l_d = 0.71·∛(E·I / w) [cm] (Δ = l/360, coefficient 1/128). I = b·d³/12.
   - l_allow = min(...). Report the governing mode. Round down to a modular step.
2. **Sheathing strip widths**: slab plywood b = 120 cm. Slab lumber b = 100 cm. Beam-side plywood b = 10 cm. Beam-side lumber b = board width (20 cm for 8").
   Ratios are what matter, since w scales with b.
3. **Two-way plate check** (เคร่า system): M = k₁wa², a = 0.14h∛(E/(k₂w)). Needs the k₁/k₂ vs b/a table from Ch. 5.
4. **Shore check**: wood Fa = 0.30E/(l/d)². Steel pipe Fa = 1×10⁷/(l/r)² for l/r ≥ 132 (else the Ch. 5 formula). P = Fa·A ≥ tributary load.
   Offer "mid-height bracing" (l/2) and "double post" options.
5. **Beam forms**: side p = 2400·h (hydrostatic). Bottom load = width × h × 2400 (per stiffener/board share). Shore load = beam concrete + tributary
   slab (half the bay to the next slab shore line, or the full tributary width for interior beams) + forms (beam 100 kg/m, slab 50 kg/m²) + LL 150 kg/m² on slab area.
   Shore/cross-beam spacing = multiple of the stud spacing.
6. **Cross-beam on double posts**: simple span between the posts, M = wl²/8, V = wl/2, w = p_bottom_total per metre × cross-beam spacing.
7. **Unit-test targets** (from the examples above): 6.1 → a = 124/77/147/78 → 75. Joist l = 80/100 → 80. Stringer 137/179/216 → 135.
   Shore Pallow = 2,745 vs 630. 6.2 → 99/71 → 70. 95/100 → 95. 153/189/224 → 150. 1,806 vs 627. 7.1 → 65/45 → 45. 106/95/197 → 90. 495 vs 414.

   7.4 → 59/43 → 40. 124/117/197 → 120. Hanger 1,200 vs 640.
